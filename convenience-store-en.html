1

She always bought radish.

2am. Every other day. He figured that out later.

The fluorescent tube in the convenience store was about to go. Third from the left. Flickered every few seconds. He'd reported it twice. The manager said next week. Last week the manager also said next week.

12:47am.

The soup in the oden pot bubbled away. Steam curled into a white line under the fluorescent light. He sat behind the register flipping through a calculus textbook, stuck on chapter three. Not because he couldn't understand it. It's that reading calculus at 1am and reading calculus during the day are not the same thing. During the day he could get through three pages. At 1am, three lines in and he'd start counting how many times the tube flickered.

The door opened. A wave of alcohol.

A man in his forties, shirt buttoned wrong, collar crooked, eyes red. Wearing slippers, left shoe untied, dragging on the floor.

"Pack of Double Happiness."

He put down the textbook, stood up, took a pack from the cigarette case and set it on the counter.

The man pulled out his phone to scan. Asked for change. He fished three coins from the drawer and slapped them on the counter.

The man paused. "Attitude problem, kid."

He said nothing. Sat back down. Went back to the textbook.

1:00. 1:15. 1:28.

2

Day two.

Lu Chen arrived at ten. Put on the apron, cleaned up after the day shift — oden cabinet unwashed, a ring of milk tea on the register, trash overflowing.

He changed the bag. Washed the cabinet. Boiled fresh broth. Cut new radish.

Calculus open on the counter. Chapter four today. Differential equations. A bit easier than integrals.

Midnight. A delivery rider bought water. Slapped change on the counter. Glanced at him. Left.

1:12am. Two drunk girls buying ice cream. Laughing loudly. He didn't look up.

1:58.

He closed the textbook. Flipped the radish in the cabinet with tongs.

2:06.

The door opened. The bell rang.

He didn't look up. But his hand stopped.

Slippers on tile. Very light. He recognized the sound. Heard it once yesterday and knew it.

She walked to the oden cabinet. A new Band-Aid on her knee. White. Neatly applied. Not the one he'd put on.

"One radish."

He stood up. Picked one out.

Half a ladle of broth. Yesterday he'd filled it full and she only sipped once. Try half today.

She took the bowl. Walked to the window. Didn't sit on the stool today. Ate standing.

He pretended to wipe the counter. Wiped the same spot three times. Watched her from the corner of his eye. She chewed slower than yesterday.

"Do you work the night shift every day?"

His hand stopped. She'd never asked him anything before. Yesterday, apart from "one radish," "how much," and "I don't owe anyone anything," those were her only three sentences.

"Mm."

"Isn't it tiring?"

"Used to it."

She didn't ask more. Looked down and ate.

He wanted to say something. Thought for a long time. Couldn't think of anything.

She finished. Bowl on the counter edge. Wrapper standing against the rim. Same as yesterday.

She walked to the door. Paused. Hand on the glass.

"Band-Aids — do you sell them here?"

"Yes. Third row on the shelf."

She didn't go get one. Turned her head and glanced at him. Smiled. A tiny smile. Her mouth barely moved.

"I mean the one you put on. Could you stick it on neater next time?"

He froze.

Next time?

She pushed the door and left. The bell rang.

He stood behind the register. The rag in his hand twisted into a knot.

Next time.

She said next time.

He looked down at the bowl on the counter. Wrapper standing. He picked it up. Still warm.

Put it in his apron pocket. The pocket had yesterday's ten-yuan bill. A pen. A tissue. Now a wrapper too.

He stared at the flickering lamp for a while.

2:27am.

Next time.

3

Day three.

When Lu Chen arrived at the store he glanced at the oden pot first. Enough radish.

He didn't know when he'd started checking whether there was enough radish.

Midnight. Changed the broth. Cut fresh radish. Two more pieces than yesterday.

Opened the math textbook to the second section on differential equations. Read half a page.

12:40am. A middle-aged woman came in for instant noodles and a sausage. He put the change on the counter — didn't slap it down. He didn't know why he didn't slap it today.

1:15am.

He put a piece of radish in a bowl. Picked one about the same size as yesterday's. Half a ladle of broth. Covered it.

Bowl on the corner of the counter. Against the register.

1:30. 1:45.

A layer of water droplets formed on the lid.

He turned a page of the textbook. Turned back.

2am.

The bell didn't ring.

2:06.

No sound of slippers on tile.

2:11.

He lifted the lid. A thin film had formed on the surface of the broth. The radish had darkened. Soaked too long.

2:20.

He carried the bowl to the sink. Poured it out.

While washing, he turned the tap on full. The water was scalding. He didn't notice.

Done. Bowl upside down on the drying rack. He stood by the sink watching water drip from the bottom of the bowl.

2:30.

He went back to the register. Sat down. Opened the textbook. Read three words. Closed it.

His phone lit up and went dark. No messages. He didn't have her number either.

2:53.

The door opened.

He stood up from the chair. Faster than usual. The book fell on the floor. He didn't pick it up.

Not her.

A delivery rider in uniform. Bought a bottle of water. Two-fifty. He slapped the change down on the counter. Snap.

The rider glanced at him. Didn't say anything. Left.

He sat back down. Picked up the book from the floor. The corner was bent.

Only the gurgling of the oden pot.

3am.

He stood up. Walked to the oden pot.

Took a bowl. Put in a piece of radish. Half a ladle of broth.

Didn't cover it.

Put it on the corner of the counter.

In the apron pocket: one skewer. The one she left yesterday. A ten-yuan note. A pen.

He looked at the door.

Not waiting for her.

Just in case.

4

Day six.

Lu Chen glanced at the step when he arrived. Screw still there. Tight.

Today she should come. Every other day. Didn't come yesterday, should come today.

He didn't know when he'd started tracking her rhythm.

Calculus at section four of differential equations. Laplace transforms. Completely lost.

12:50am. An old man in slippers buying soy sauce. He set the change on the counter. Didn't slap it.

He noticed he didn't slap it.

1:30. Changed the broth. Cut fresh radish. Stood behind the counter watching the radish sink and float.

2:14.

The door opened.

When she pushed through the door he knew something was wrong.

She didn't walk to the oden cabinet. Stood at the entrance. Looking at him. Eyes red. Not from crying. The kind of red from not sleeping for a long time. Eyelids a bit swollen. Lips dry.

"What happened?"

He spoke. Two words. He didn't expect to say them himself. In three days he hadn't asked her a single personal question.

She shook her head. Walked over slowly. Slower than the past few days.

She sat in the folding chair beside the register. His chair. The cushion shaped like him. She tilted when she sat. Same as the first day.

"Don't want to eat."

He stood there watching her for a while. She was looking down. Fingers twisted together. Nails cut short.

He didn't say anything. Walked behind the oden cabinet. Picked up a piece of radish. Half a ladle of broth. Set it on the counter beside her.

She glanced at the bowl. Didn't take it.

He walked to the door side and turned off two rows of lights. The switches clicked twice. The store went half dark. The oden cabinet's glow still on, casting a small patch of warm light.

She sat on the line between the lit half and the dark half.

A few minutes passed. She picked up the bowl and sipped the broth. Set it down. Sipped again.

Didn't eat the radish.

He pretended to organize the shelves behind the counter. Stacked the water bottles once. Then again.

He heard the bowl set down. Very softly.

Turned to look — she was leaning back, eyes closed. A trace of broth at the corner of her mouth.

Asleep.

He walked over. Her hand resting on the armrest. Phone in her grip. Screen dark.

The phone case was clear. A photo tucked inside.

He bent down and glanced.

A young woman. Early twenties. Short hair. Smiling. A rapeseed field behind her.

He didn't look again. Straightened up. Walked to the AC panel. Twenty-two degrees. He set it to twenty-four.

He walked to the shelves. Sale section. Took a thin blanket. Thirty-nine ninety-nine. Pink. Ugly. A cartoon bear on the packaging.

He tore off the packaging. Unfolded it. Walked to her side.

Draped it over her legs. Very carefully. Afraid she'd wake.

She stirred. Her mouth moved like she was saying something. Didn't wake.

He went back behind the counter. Sat down. Watched her.

The oden cabinet's light on her face. Her eyelashes were long, casting a small shadow. Dark circles under her eyes. The broth stain at her mouth had dried.

3am.

He opened the textbook. Read one line. Closed it.

3:15.

She turned over. The blanket slipped. He stood up, walked over, and pulled it back up.

3:30.

She didn't wake. He didn't wake her.

The oden cabinet gurgling. The lamp flickered once. He stared at that dying lamp for a while.

4:20.

Her phone buzzed. Vibrating. Jumping on the armrest.

She jolted awake. Saw the ceiling. Saw the fluorescent light. Saw herself in a convenience store.

Saw the blanket on her legs. Pink.

Saw him. Behind the counter. Wiping.

Froze for two seconds.

"How long was I asleep?"

"Two hours."

She looked down at the blanket. Picked it up. Checked the tag.

"Thirty-nine ninety-nine?"

"On sale."

"Ugly."

"Mm."

She folded the blanket. Very neatly. Set it on the chair.

Took out her phone. Screen lit up. Her face changed.

"I have to go."

She stood. The bowl still on the counter. Radish still there. Broth gone cold.

Walked to the door. Looked back at him.

"The blanket..."

"Return it next time."

Her mouth moved. Tiny. Same smile as the past few days.

Left. The bell rang.

He watched her walk. Left. No umbrella. The rain had stopped.

Back at the counter. The radish in the bowl had gone cold. He didn't dump it.

Twelve wrappers in his pocket. A ten-yuan bill. A pen.

He picked up the bowl. Sipped the cold broth.

At 2:31 when she came the broth was hot. At 4:22 when she left it was cold.

He finished the broth. Didn't eat the radish.

5:30. Off shift.

He walked out. Stood on the step. Screw still there. Tight.

Walked right. He always walked right.

5

Day eight.

After his shift Lu Chen went to the pharmacy. Not for himself.

Band-Aids. White ones. Same brand as hers. He searched the shelf for a minute — he didn't know there were so many kinds. Waterproof, breathable, cartoon, skin-colored. Hers were the most basic white gauze Band-Aids. Two fifty a box.

He bought two boxes.

At the store he put them in the first aid kit. The kit was under the counter now. No need to bend down and search. Reach and grab.

Calculus at Laplace transforms section two. Still lost. But today he got through three pages. Don't know why. Maybe nothing to do with Laplace.

1:50. Radish cut for the oden cabinet. Three extra pieces today. He put one in a bowl. Half a ladle of broth. Set it in the corner of the counter.

No lid.

He looked at the bowl. Thought about it. Still no lid.

2:07.

The door opened. The bell loud in the quiet store.

She walked in. No rain today. Hair dry. Low ponytail. First time he'd seen her tie her hair.

But the dark circles under her eyes were deeper. Lips still dry.

She walked to the counter. Glanced at the bowl in the corner. Glanced at him.

"Heat me a fresh one."

He didn't say anything.

"That one's been sitting too long," she said.

She knew he'd prepared it ahead. He didn't know when she'd noticed.

He dumped the old bowl. Washed it. Picked fresh radish. Half a ladle of broth. Handed it over.

She took the bowl. Walked to the window. Sat down today. On the stool.

Ate slowly. Slower than usual.

He pretended to read calculus. Read the same sentence twice.

"Who's the person in your phone case?"

He didn't expect to ask. The textbook nearly slid off the counter.

She stopped. Radish in her mouth, unchewed. Eyes on the broth surface.

A few seconds. She finished chewing. Swallowed. Set the bowl down. Pushed it away a little.

"My mom."

Two words. That was it. No more.

He waited.

"The photo was taken when she was twenty-three." She spoke again. Voice different. Lower. "Back home. Rapeseed fields. She'd just gotten married that year."

He didn't say anything. Set the rag down. Stopped wiping.

She looked out the window. The parking lot. The street light. Same direction as always. Left.

"She's been in the hospital for three years."

Her voice was flat. Like talking about the weather. Like whether it would rain.

"Brain hemorrhage. Not a vegetable but close. Sometimes her fingers move. The doctor says it's probably just reflexes."

She paused.

"I don't think so."

He stood behind the counter. Hands on the edge. Didn't know what to say. Didn't know if he should say anything.

"My dad left the first year. Couldn't take it."

Her tone didn't change. Still flat.

"I don't blame him. Who could take it."

She picked up the bowl. Sipped. The broth was still warm.

"I'm on night shift every other day. On the off nights I can't sleep at 2am. Walk out of the apartment. Twenty-two minutes. Keep walking and end up here."

She looked at him. First time she'd looked at him for more than two seconds on purpose.

"Your radish isn't good."

His mouth twitched. Almost laughed. Held it.

"Then why do you come every day."

"At 2am this is the only place open."

He wanted to say there were other places open at 2am in Panyu. Didn't.

She set the bowl on the counter. Wrapper standing. A bit of broth left.

Stood up. Pulled her jacket.

"The broth's cold. Let me get you a fresh one."

"No need."

She walked to the door. Hand on the glass.

Paused.

He looked at her back. Gray jacket. Shoulders narrower than he'd thought.

She didn't turn around.

"See you tomorrow."

Not the day after tomorrow.

Tomorrow.

He stood behind the counter. The textbook slid off and hit the floor. He didn't pick it up.

Tomorrow.

She said tomorrow.

Fourteen wrappers in his pocket. A ten-yuan bill. A pen. Two boxes of Band-Aids in the first aid kit.

The oden cabinet gurgling. The lamp flickered. He stared at that dying lamp.

Smiled.

2:29am.

Tomorrow.

Day nine.

When Lu Chen arrived at the store he did something he'd never done before — he restocked the corn in the oden pot.

Corn wasn't on the manager's order form. He'd bought it himself. Spent twelve yuan at the wholesale market that afternoon on a bag. Carried it to the store. Cut it up and put it in.

He didn't know if she'd eat it. But she'd been eating radish for eight days. Time for a change.

The math textbook was open to the differential equations review. Did two problems. Got one right. The other one he realized halfway through he'd gotten the sign wrong.

Midnight. 1am.

She came.

Not every other day anymore. Yesterday she came. Today too. Yesterday she said "see you tomorrow."

2:04am. Three minutes earlier than yesterday.

Lu Chen heard the bell while reading Laplace transforms. Today he got through five pages. He didn't know why he could focus lately.

She walked to the oden pot. Stood there a moment.

"There's something new today."

He leaned out from behind the counter.

"Corn. Put it in this afternoon."

She glanced at him.

"You didn't used to sell corn."

"Trying it out."

She picked up a piece of radish. Hesitated. Then half an ear of corn.

He stood up. Took out bowls. Two bowls.

"Separate. The broth is different."

She looked at the two bowls. Corner of her mouth moved.

"When did you get so particular."

He didn't answer. Radish bowl, half a ladle of broth. Corn bowl, no broth.

She carried both bowls to the window. Sat down today. On the high stool.

Tried the corn first. Chewed slowly.

"How is it."

He asked from behind the counter. Voice a little louder than usual. He noticed. Cleared his throat.

"Not bad."

When she said not bad she was smiling. Not the twitch-of-the-mouth kind. Her eyes were smiling.

She finished the corn. Finished the radish. Both bowls empty.

Bowls on the counter. Two skewers standing up. Two. One more than before.

"Will you have it tomorrow."

"Yes."

When he said yes he was already thinking about cutting more corn tomorrow.

She left. Didn't turn around. But at the door she said —

"Corn is better than radish."

The bell rang.

He stood behind the counter. Collected both bowls. Washed them. When he washed the corn bowl the water ran for a long time.

Fifteen skewers in his pocket.

He took out his phone. Opened the delivery app. Searched for where to buy corn wholesale nearby.

Twelve yuan a bag. Buy another one tomorrow.
