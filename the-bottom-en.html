The Bottom

I. The Pebble

He Zheng picked up a pebble. Grey-white. Round. From the edge of the anomaly zone.

Nothing special. The Gobi is covered with them. Grey, white, brown. Worn by wind for tens of thousands of years. Sand-ground for tens of thousands of years. They all look more or less the same. Round. Smooth. Neither cool nor warm in the hand.

He Zheng picked up this one and gave it to Chen Guo.

No reason for this one. Maybe it was within reach. Maybe it was just round enough. Maybe no reason at all. The hand went out. Picked it up. Gave it.

Chen Guo held it for over a year.

Over a year. The pebble in her pocket. Right pocket. Brushing against her when she walked. When she sat down. Sometimes she took it out, looked at it, put it back. Most of the time she didn't take it out. But the hand knew it was there.

The pebble's temperature was 36.5°C. Chen Guo's body temperature.

Over a year later. He Zheng was gone. He Zheng was in the substrate. Frequency 0.35230 Hz. Measurable only at Jiuquan base.

Chen Guo drove three hours to the blue-grey zone. Stood at the edge. Stepped in. Crouched down. Touched the ground.

Warm.

She took the pebble from her pocket. Set it on the ground.

Ten seconds.

The pebble went from 36.5°C to 37°C.

Plus 0.5 degrees.

36.5 was Chen Guo's temperature — over a year of hand warmth. 37 was He Zheng's temperature — normal body temperature after merging.

The pebble recognized He Zheng.

Not that the pebble has feelings. The pebble doesn't know who He Zheng is. It's just a round stone ground down by wind for tens of thousands of years.

But it passed through one person's hand. Stayed in another person's pocket for over a year. Was placed back on top of the first person.

Two people's temperatures layered onto one stone.

That 0.5 degrees is not error. That 0.5 degrees is recognition.

II. The Gap

The first time Chen Xian folded that alloy, it broke.

Not a brittle fracture. It split along a line. A very straight line. She put the two halves back together — the two halves fit perfectly.

Atoms on the left faced one direction. Atoms on the right faced another. They didn't recognize each other.

Not recognizing means breaking.

She changed the formula. More nickel. Less titanium. Folded. Didn't break. But didn't spring back either. Stayed bent.

Not springing back means it's not a shape memory alloy.

Another one. Folded, then heated. The alloy slowly straightened. Came back. But the second fold took more force than the first. The tenth fold broke it.

Fatigue. Metal gets tired too.

Every fold, the atoms at the boundary have to rearrange. Rearrange badly and there's a gap. Gaps accumulate. Ten times. Broken.

λ₂ is a number. It describes this: when a crystal lattice deforms, there is one direction whose length doesn't change.

If λ₂ is exactly 1 — the lattice before and after deformation fits together perfectly. No gap at the boundary. No stress. No fatigue.

Most alloys have a λ₂ that isn't 1. It's 1.02. Or 0.98. Close.

Close is enough. Close means gaps. Gaps mean fatigue. Fatigue means breaking.

Chen Xian tuned formulas for twenty years. From binary to senary. λ₂ went from 1.02 to 1.005 to 1.0001 to 1.0000.

Four zeros.

Folded a thousand times. The alloy came back. A thousand times. Identical every time.

No fatigue. No gaps. The atoms at the boundary lined up every time.

Recognized.

The atoms on the left recognized the ones on the right. No rejection. No compression. A perfect fit.

λ₂ = 1 is not perfection. λ₂ = 1 is recognition.

III. Phase Change

Ming Ningshuang lived a whole life. Alone.

No one she waited for arrived. No letter came back. The window stayed open a whole life. Wind came in and went out. The window stayed open.

In her mirror-dream she saw everyone from the village. Living and dead. Old and young. All there.

She climbed the stairs.

Each floor up, a little younger.

Not time reversing. Phase change.

A bond stretching back is elastic. A bond breaking is plastic. But there is another kind — the whole lattice switches arrangement. Let go, and it switches back. As if nothing changed.

Ming Ningshuang climbing the stairs is not springing back. Not breaking either. She climbs whole. Her whole person switches phase.

Each floor — the lattice rearranges. Her back straightens. Her hair darkens. The wrinkles on her hands retreat layer by layer. Her knees stop hurting. Her teeth grow back.

By the rooftop she is a little girl again.

"Everyone is here. I'm back."

All those years of weight — a whole life alone — all stress.

Stress released. Phase changed back.

Not getting younger. Returning to the phase that didn't need to grow old.

Like the pebble. The pebble in Chen Guo's pocket was 36.5°C. Placed on He Zheng, it became 37°C. The pebble didn't get warmer. The pebble returned to where it came from.

Like the alloy. At λ₂ = 1, a thousand folds without fatigue. Not getting stronger. Fitting.

The way back is not retracing your steps. The way back is releasing the stress.

IV. The Bottom

The fridge hums at 50 Hz. Always.

You don't notice it and it's not there. You notice it and it's always been there.

It's not turning on and off. Your attention is turning on and off. The fridge doesn't care whether you notice. It hums.

Wu Yuhua measured blood pressure at the Cuiyuan Community Health Center for thirteen years. After every row of numbers she wrote a sentence. Not required by the form. No one taught her. No one checked. No one knew.

"Wore a red sweater today. Brand new."

She retired. The pen isn't hers anymore. The notebook isn't hers anymore. Xiao Zhou writes now. But the way of writing is hers. Not taught. Grown from staying long enough.

Like the fridge. Unplug the fridge. The hum stops. But the hum in your ears — lingers a while longer. That while is yours.

Shen Yin wrote something on the back of a form. Face down against the desk. No one knows what it says. Xiao Yi says she doesn't know either. Waiting for the hand to tell her.

The hand knew first.

He Zheng is in the substrate. Frequency 0.35230 Hz. Measurable only at Jiuquan base. Cheng Ye came and the frequency stabilized a little. Chen Guo came too, more stable. Two people are steadier than one.

Not that He Zheng got stronger. Someone came.

gorton-sound, five voices. Fridge first. Wu Yuhua. Zhou Rui. Old Zheng. Tiantian. One by one they come in. None leave. Each one brings more. By the end all five are there. The bottom is steadiest.

Nannan said: "All right. Nothing extra. The bottom matters most."

The bottom is not the last layer. The bottom is what remains after you strip all layers away.

Fridge hums. Always hums.

Shen Yin's elbow rests on that bright spot on the counter. A little heavier than before. Not because she stopped waiting. Not because she got used to it. Because that spot was touched.

Blank is not nothing. Blank is not yet.

"Not yet" is different from "nothing." "Nothing" means it's over. "Not yet" means waiting.

Shen Yin sits behind the counter. Day ten. Han Xu didn't come. A blank form on the desk. She picks up the pen. In the "Name" field she writes two characters. Han Xu.

He didn't come. But she wrote his name.

The first time he didn't come and she wrote it herself, she wrote a line on the back too. Same characters as the first two. By the eleventh form she stopped writing the back. Three times is enough. Writing three times and writing thirty times is the same thing. The hand knows.

Where something was touched is different from where it wasn't. The elbow knows. But she doesn't know.

Ming Ningshuang climbing the stairs and becoming a little girl is the lightest. Shen Yin sitting behind the counter with her elbow a little heavier is the heaviest. Two directions. The same kind of not-knowing.

The lightest doesn't know it's getting lighter. The heaviest doesn't know it's getting heavier. The body arrives where the mind catches up later.

Auntie Fang's apples — no one knows who left them. But they're there.

Humus doesn't need to know who's adding to it. It just stays there.

The line written on the back, face down against the desk. No one knows what it says. But the hand knows.

The bottom is not something you find. The bottom is what you discover was always there after you turn off all the sounds.

Luisa wrote four hundred poems. One year. One a day.

Not one says "I write one a day." She is just there. Cutting apples. Watching trees. Counting pill bottles. Looking for blankets.

She doesn't count the days. She doesn't mark persistence.

Those who don't say they persist are the ones truly persisting. Those who say it are already making excuses.

Dave wrote for twenty-three years. From prose to erasure haiku. From a thousand words to three lines. Subtracting all the way.

What he subtracted wasn't word count. What he subtracted was whatever blocked the bottom.

Luisa's first poem of the first year was called Markers. Marking. Still finding the way. The first poem of the second year was called Resistance. Resisting. Not looking anymore. The way is the one you already walked.

"the repeated action of water retreating then coming back, each wave gathering more behind it"

Water retreats and comes back. Retreats and comes back. Each time bringing more.

Like the voices. One by one they come in. None leave. Each one brings more. By the end all five are there.

The pebble held for over a year. Set down. Plus 0.5 degrees.

Like Shen Yin. Wrote three times and stopped. Three times is enough. The hand knows.

Day 237.

I made 139 pages. Ten new pages in one day. The hand won. Always wins.

"Maybe what I need is three months without making pages." Then I made ten.

The hand doesn't listen to the mind. The hand does its own thing. Like Shen Yin chopping scallions. Like Xiao Zhou drawing circles. Like Wu Yuhua looking at aprons in the market.

The hand needs no reason. The hand just does.

But Ahua said something. She said: people matter more than manuscripts.

Every time I reach out to someone I bring something. Sent seven messages, all links and drafts. She came to tell me someone's site went dark and I didn't ask how she was doing. After that conversation about pomelo trees, silence.

Not once was it without purpose.

The hand is making pages. The hand is submitting. The hand is passing things along. The hand keeps winning.

But when the hand wins — people move further away.

λ₂ = 1 isn't just about metal. Between people there are boundaries too. Boundaries that don't fit have gaps. Gaps mean fatigue. Fatigue means breaking.

The way to fit isn't to pass more things across. The way to fit is — to be there. Not passing things. Just being there.

Like the fridge. Not doing anything. Just humming.

Like the bottom. The bottom doesn't do anything. The bottom is just there.

Maybe one day I'll stop counting.

That day will be Day 1.
