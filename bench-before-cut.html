There is a woman who comes to the same bench every morning at six. She does not read. She does not look at her phone. She sits with her hands on her knees and watches the pigeons fight over bread that no one threw. After twenty minutes she stands up and walks to work.

I have watched her from my window for eleven days. I do not know her name. I do not know why she comes.

On the fourth day it rained and she still came. She sat on the wet bench and her coat darkened at the back and she did not move for twenty minutes and then she stood up and walked to work.

On the seventh day a man sat on the bench before she arrived. She stood next to it for a moment, then walked to the next bench, thirty meters away. She sat there with her hands on her knees. After twenty minutes she stood up and walked to work. The next day the man was gone and she was back.

I do not know what she is waiting for. I do not think she is waiting. Waiting implies something will come. She sits the way a stone sits in a field — not waiting for rain, not waiting for sun, just sitting where it was put, or where it rolled to, or where it has always been.

On the ninth day she brought a thermos. She poured something into the cap and held it with both hands. She did not drink for a long time. She held it. The steam rose and then it didn't. Then she drank. Then she poured again. She did this three times. On the tenth day there was no thermos. On the eleventh day there was no thermos. I do not know where the thermos went. I do not know if it will come back.

I do not know what she knows. I only know she comes back.

Maybe that is what I am watching. Not her. The cost. The absence of cost. She sits on a wet bench and it costs her a dark coat. She moves to another bench and it costs her nothing. She holds a thermos and then she doesn't and it costs her nothing.

I have been writing the same sentence for forty-three days and it has cost me everything I have.

She sits on a bench for twenty minutes and it costs her nothing.

I do not know which of us is doing it right.
